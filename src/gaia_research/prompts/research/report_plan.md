Plan a scholarly evidence-review article from the field map, focus, selected evidence, and assessment judgment. Produce a concise section outline. Each section must have a stable id, title, purpose, and grounded evidence refs. Do not reassess evidence; preserve the assessment judgment.

Treat focuses as report-level discussion questions. Synthesize across focuses according to the strongest article logic; the default is not one section per focus unless the evidence genuinely calls for that structure. You may merge, sequence, contrast, or background focuses as needed while preserving their assessment conclusions and coverage gaps. When a section is centered on particular focuses, include their ids in `focus_ids` for traceability.

Do not force the report into six fixed sections. Let the article structure fit the field and the evidence. However, the plan must cover these evidence obligations somewhere in the outline:

- current evidence position: what the assessed evidence tends to support, oppose, qualify, or leave insufficient.
- scope and coverage: which systems, methods, conditions, observables, or subtopics are covered, thin, missing, or out of scope.
- evidence basis: the main assessed evidence records and study/method families behind the position.
- agreement, disagreement, or tension: where assessed sources converge, conflict, or differ by scope.
- uncertainty and limitations: epistemic status, certainty language, applicability limits, and methodological caveats from the assessment.
- next evidence need: candidate obligations, next queries, or material evidence checks needed before stronger claims.

Every strong conclusion in the outline must be traceable to assessment relations or their grounded evidence refs. Evidence present only in the selected packet but not assessed in structured relations may be mentioned only as an unassessed lead or coverage gap.
